How to find your own breathing pace

Six breaths a minute is an average. Research on resonance breathing describes each person's own pace as sitting a little either side, often between about 4.5 and 7 breaths a minute. Finding yours takes a few short sessions.

A simple way to compare

  1. Pick a quiet time of day and keep it the same for the whole comparison.
  2. Breathe for five minutes at one pace a day. Start at 6, then try 5.5, 6.5, 5 and 7 on the following days.
  3. After each session, score two things from 1 to 5: how comfortable the pace felt, and how settled you feel now.
  4. Choose the pace with the best scores. If two tie, choose the slower one only if it felt just as comfortable.

With a pulse reading

If you can see your heart rate beat by beat, look for the pace where it rises and falls most, and most smoothly, with each breath. Research describes that as your resonance pace. Give each pace a couple of minutes to settle before you judge it. Why the heart rate follows the breath.

Signs a pace is too slow for you today

  • You feel short of air, or you gasp at the start of each in-breath.
  • Your breaths get bigger to keep up.
  • You feel light-headed. Stop and breathe normally.

Move half a breath a minute faster and try again. Many people find slower paces easier after a few weeks of practice, so it's worth comparing again later.
