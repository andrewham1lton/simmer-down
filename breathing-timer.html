10 seconds a breath: 4 in, 6 out.

How to use it

  1. Sit or lie comfortably and let your shoulders drop.
  2. Breathe in through your nose as the circle grows. Let your belly move more than your chest.
  3. Breathe out slowly as it shrinks, through your nose or through softly pursed lips.
  4. Keep each breath small and smooth. Slow breathing doesn't need big breaths.

With a screen reader, the timer says "Breathe in" and "Breathe out" as each part of the breath begins, so you can close your eyes and listen. If you feel light-headed, stop and breathe normally. Breathing in too deeply is the usual reason.

Why six breaths a minute

Your heart rate rises a little as you breathe in and falls as you breathe out. Research on paced breathing describes that rise and fall as largest, for many adults, at around six breaths a minute. Your own pace may sit a little either side, which is why this timer goes from 4.5 to 7. How to find your own pace.

Choosing the balance

The same length in and out is the easiest place to start. Many slow-breathing practices make the out-breath a little longer, such as 4 seconds in and 6 out, and many people find that more settling. Twice as long out suits slower paces once the rhythm feels comfortable. More on a longer out-breath.

What this page does with your data

Where Simmer Down fits

This timer keeps the pace. Simmer Down adds your own heart rhythm: rest a fingertip over your iPhone's rear camera, follow the slow breathing guide, and watch your heart rate rise as you breathe in and ease as you breathe out.

Or put the phone down and breathe hands-free, led by gentle taps. The two-minute Reset is free for good.
