An evening wind-down breathing routine

Slow breathing in the evening gives the day a clear end. Many people do it in bed with the lights low, as the last thing before they turn over to sleep.

Before you start

  • Dim the lights, or turn them off if you'll do it lying down.
  • Turn on Do Not Disturb or your Sleep Focus so nothing lights up the screen.
  • Lie on your back with a pillow under your knees, or sit propped up against the headboard.
  • Rest one hand on your belly so you can feel each breath.

The routine: ten minutes

  1. Minutes 1 and 2. Breathe normally. Each time you breathe out, let the breath go a little longer, without forcing it.
  2. Minutes 3 to 8. Breathe in through your nose for 4 and out for 6. That's six breaths a minute. Feel your hand rise and fall.
  3. Minutes 9 and 10. Stop counting. Keep the breath slow and quiet, and let it drift back to its own pace.

If 4 and 6 feels too slow tonight, use 3 and 5. Try the paces first with the free breathing timer so you know them by feel.

Following a pace in the dark

A bright screen is the opposite of what you want at the end of the day. Count in your head, or use something you can hear or feel, such as a low tone or a vibration, so you can close your eyes. If you use a phone, set it face down or turn the brightness all the way down first.

Keep it comfortable

There are no holds in this routine, and every breath should feel comfortable. If your mind wanders, come back to the count. If you fall asleep before the ten minutes are up, that's fine. A longer out-breath is the core of it, and breathing out longer than you breathe in explains why many practices use one.
