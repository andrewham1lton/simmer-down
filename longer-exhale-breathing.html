Breathing out longer than you breathe in

Many slow-breathing practices make the out-breath longer than the in-breath. A common pattern is 4 seconds in and 6 out. Many people find a longer out-breath feels more settling than an even one.

Why the out-breath

Your heart rate eases a little on every out-breath and lifts on every in-breath. Why that happens. A longer out-breath spends more of each breath in the easing part. It also slows the whole breath down without asking you to take in more air.

Ratios people use

In to outExamplePace
Even, 1 to 15 in, 5 out6 breaths a minute
2 to 34 in, 6 out6 breaths a minute
1 to 24 in, 8 out5 breaths a minute

Start with even breaths if slow breathing is new to you, then move to 2 to 3 once the rhythm feels comfortable. Twice as long out suits people who already find six a minute comfortable.

How to lengthen it without straining

  • Breathe out through softly pursed lips, as if cooling a spoonful of soup. The small opening slows the air.
  • Or narrow the back of your throat a little and breathe out through your nose, so the out-breath makes a faint sound.
  • Stop when the breath runs out naturally. You don't need to empty your lungs.
  • Keep the in-breath the same size. A bigger in-breath to have more air to let out works against you.

Two other ways to slow the out-breath

Humming. Hum softly on each out-breath. The hum slows the air on its own, and the length of the hum tells you how long the out-breath was.

A sigh. Take two short breaths in through your nose, the second on top of the first, then one long, slow breath out through your mouth. Many people do this without thinking at the end of a tense moment. One or two is enough before you go back to even breathing.
