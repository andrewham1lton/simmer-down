What resonance breathing is

Resonance breathing is slow, smooth breathing at the pace where your heart rate and your breath move together most strongly. For many adults that pace is around six breaths a minute: about 4 seconds in and 6 seconds out, or 5 and 5.

Where the name comes from

Your heart rate already rises a little each time you breathe in and falls each time you breathe out. Your body also adjusts your heart rate in a slower wave of its own, about once every ten seconds, as part of the reflex that balances your circulation.

Research on paced breathing describes what happens when you breathe at about that same pace: the two waves line up and add together, and the rise and fall in heart rate grows much larger than at your usual pace. It works like pushing a swing in time with its own rhythm. That matching of rhythms is the resonance.

Other names for it

You'll also see it called coherence breathing, coherent breathing, resonant breathing, HRV breathing (after heart rate variability) or simply six breaths a minute. The details differ a little from teacher to teacher. The core is the same: slow, even breaths with no holds, at around six a minute.

How to try it

  1. Sit upright but relaxed, with your feet on the floor and your hands resting in your lap.
  2. Breathe in through your nose for about 4 seconds and let your belly expand.
  3. Breathe out for about 6 seconds without pushing. Let the air leave on its own.
  4. Go straight into the next in-breath. There are no pauses or holds.
  5. Keep going for five to ten minutes. Two minutes is a fine start.

Counting in your head works, but a pacer is easier to follow. The free breathing timer on this site runs at six breaths a minute by default.

What it feels like at first

Six breaths a minute is slower than most people breathe at rest, so the first few minutes can feel like you aren't getting enough air. Make each breath smaller rather than bigger. If you feel light-headed, stop and breathe normally, since breathing too deeply is the usual cause. Many people find the pace comfortable after a few sessions.

Your own resonance pace

Six is an average. Each person's pace sits a little either side, usually somewhere between about 4.5 and 7 breaths a minute. How to find yours. To see why the heart rate moves with the breath at all, read why your heart rate rises when you breathe in.
