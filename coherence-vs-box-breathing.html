Coherence breathing or box breathing

Both are slow, counted breathing, and both are simple to learn. The difference is the holds. Box breathing pauses after the in-breath and after the out-breath. Coherence breathing never pauses.

Box breathingCoherence breathing
PatternIn 4, hold 4, out 4, hold 4In about 4 to 5, out about 5 to 6
PaceAbout 4 breaths a minute at a count of 4 secondsAbout 6 breaths a minute
HoldsTwo in every breathNone
What you attend toThe count and the four sides of the squareOne smooth, continuous wave
Heart rateThe pauses break the rise and fall into stepsAims for the largest, smoothest rise and fall

How to do box breathing

  1. Breathe out fully.
  2. Breathe in through your nose for a count of 4.
  3. Hold, without straining, for 4.
  4. Breathe out for 4.
  5. Hold with your lungs empty for 4, then start again.

If the holds feel tight, shorten them to 2 and keep the in-breath and out-breath at 4.

How to do coherence breathing

  1. Breathe in through your nose for about 4 seconds.
  2. Breathe out slowly for about 6 seconds.
  3. Turn straight into the next in-breath, with no pause at the top or the bottom.

A pacer makes it easier to keep the rhythm. The free breathing timer on this site does it in your browser.

Why the holds matter

Your heart rate rises as you breathe in and falls as you breathe out. Research on paced breathing describes that rise and fall as largest when the breath flows smoothly at around six breaths a minute, which is the idea behind coherence breathing. During a hold, the wave stalls. More on resonance breathing.

Which to choose

Many people pick box breathing for a minute or two before a stressful moment, because the count gives the mind something to hold on to. Coherence breathing suits a longer, settled session of five to ten minutes, where you follow the breath rather than the count. If you can't decide, try each for a week and keep the one you return to.
